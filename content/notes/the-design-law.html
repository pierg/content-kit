The design law

Status: LIVE — a layer without a mechanical check is a suggestion; every check ships with a fixture designed to break it.

A rule nothing checks is a suggestion, and suggestions drift.

Not through bad faith. A rule written in prose is read once and applied from memory, and it is lost the first time an agent is in a hurry or a new author never read it. A rule in the gate is applied on every commit by something that does not forget. So every layer of content-kit answers the question what checks it? The status line is linted. A genre's shape is checked through its proxies: a word bound, a ban on sections, a required definition. The shell's vocabulary is checked token by token and class by class. The indices are compared with the content, never rewritten. An open annotation is held to its passage. Where a layer cannot be checked, as with a genre's register, it says so outright and leaves it to a reader, instead of dressing judgment up as a rule.

The second half keeps the first honest. A check that has never been seen to fail may not work at all: a pattern that never matches, a function nothing calls, a check a genre names that no module implements. So a check ships with a fixture designed to break it, a planted page with a known answer, and it is not a check until that fixture fails by name. The engine's own gate (its selftest and its end-to-end run) plants one for every core check and every extension point, and a genre that names a check nobody provides stops the gate instead of silently passing.

The law cuts both ways. It forbids pretending a convention is enforced when nothing enforces it, and it forbids adding a check without proving it can fail. How to hold your own checks to it is in The gate, §4; where a new check belongs is the placement rule.
