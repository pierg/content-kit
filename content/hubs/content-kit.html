content-kit

The rules, the checks and the review loop that keep a library of agent-written pages consistent. Start here.

What it is

Agents can already write a library's pages. What they do not do on their own is keep the pages consistent: each in the right place and the right voice, passing checks anyone can rerun, and open to review by a person reading the page in a browser, not a diff.

content-kit is the smallest set of tools that makes this hold. A genre gives a page its structure and its voice. The gate, ckit check, checks what a machine can check, and leaves the rest, such as tone, to readers. One shell gives every page the same look. An annotation keeps a reader's comment in a file until an agent answers it.

It is not a site generator. Each page is HTML its author wrote, served exactly as written: nothing converts it, and ckit export only copies it for a static host. Why hand-authored HTML argues for that choice and counts its costs. One law runs through the rest: a rule nothing checks is a suggestion. This site is itself a content-kit library, checked by the command it documents.

uv tool install git+https://github.com/pierg/content-kit@v0.5.0
ckit init my-library --name "My library" && cd my-library
ckit new concept feedback-loop --title "Feedback loop"
ckit lint && make check && make docs

Open questions

  • Is a proxy enough? The gate checks a genre's shape (its length, its sections, a definition) and leaves its tone to readers. No one has measured whether such checks make agent-written pages better than written guidance alone: not here, and not, as far as the prior art shows, anywhere.
  • Pages that repeat the record. A page that copies a fact from the record can go out of date while every check passes. Nothing yet compares a page's statements with the lines they came from. Today that is a writing rule, not a check.
  • The paper itself. The paper genre covers a paper's landing page. Its .tex source is outside the gate: its status, its figures, its citations, and an abstract that matches the page.
