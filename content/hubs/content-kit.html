content-kit

Status: LIVE — a page contract for agent-written knowledge: where a page goes, how it reads, what checks it, and how a person reviews it in the browser. Start here.

Current take

Agents can already write a library's pages. What they do not do unaided is keep those pages belonging: in the right place, in the right voice, passing a check anyone can rerun, and open to review by someone reading the rendered page rather than a diff. content-kit is the smallest set of mechanisms that makes that hold. A genre carries a structure and a voice; a gate checks what can be checked and says plainly what cannot; one shell gives every page the same vocabulary; an annotation lets a reader's note wait in a file until an agent answers it.

It is not a site generator. Pages are HTML written by their author and served exactly as written, with no build in between, and that choice is argued, costs included, in Why hand-authored HTML. It holds itself to one law: a rule nothing checks is a suggestion. This site is itself a content-kit library, gated by the same command it documents.

uv tool install git+https://github.com/pierg/content-kit@v0.4.0
ckit init my-library --name "My library" && cd my-library
ckit new concept feedback-loop --title "Feedback loop"
ckit lint && make check && make docs

Open questions

  • Is a proxy enough? The gate checks a genre's shape (length, sections, a definition) and leaves its register to readers. Whether mechanical proxies make agent-written pages better than written guidance alone has not been measured, here or, as far as the prior art shows, anywhere.
  • Pages that restate the record. A page that copies a fact from the record can go stale with the gate green; nothing yet compares a page's statements with the lines they came from. Today that is a writing rule, not a check.
  • The paper itself. The paper genre covers a paper's landing page. Its .tex source (status, figures, citations, the abstract matching the page) is outside the gate.
