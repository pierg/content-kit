RECORD — definition of record
The record is the set of markdown files a repository declares in kit.json under record (a field log, a decision log, a changelog): files that grow by appending and say how the work got where it is. The shell renders each one read-only at /shell/record.html?p=<path> and lists it in the sidebar. Pages under the content directory say where the work stands, and link into the record rather than copy from it.

Why it matters

A library mixes content that changes at different speeds. A concept holds for years; a decision log gains a line a week. Put a week's fact into a year's page and the page is wrong the next week, with nothing to say so. Keeping the record in its own files, and letting pages only point at it, means a fact about the past has one home and every reader reaches the same copy.

The record stays markdown on purpose: it is written quickly, often by hand, in the order things happen, and it is read far less than it is written. It is not a genre and the lint does not hold it to one. What the engine does with it is render it in the shell, list its files in the sidebar, copy them into an exported site, and build the chronicle from its dated headings. That a file is only ever appended to is a convention of the people keeping it; the engine does not enforce it.

Worked example

A bird-watching library keeps one log per year and declares it:

"record": ["log/2026.md"]
# Field log 2026 — LIVE

## 2026-04-12 — [decision] count only birds seen, not heard

Heard-only records made the spring totals incomparable with last year's, so from today a bird counts once someone has seen it.

### 2026-05-03 — first swifts over the reservoir

Eleven at dusk, feeding low.

The sidebar gains a Record group with "Field log 2026 — LIVE" in it (the title is the file's first heading). A chapter about counting method does not restate the rule and its date; it says the club counts only birds seen and links the reason, /shell/record.html?p=log/2026.md#2026-04-12-count-only-birds-seen-not-heard, where the rule was decided.

record names files a reader should browse. A repository whose record is a whole directory names that directory under chronicle.sources instead, which the chronicle sweeps but the sidebar does not list; when chronicle.sources is absent the chronicle falls back to record.

Common trap

Transcribing the record into a page. Copying "eleven swifts on 3 May" into a concept or a hub feels helpful and is how pages go stale: the log gets a correction, the page does not, and no check compares the two. A page that needs a fact from the record cites the line that holds it. Every number carries its source, and is taken from that source rather than from another page.

Related concepts

The chronicle is the timeline generated from the record. A repository with its own record conventions reads them through an extractor, one kind of extension point.
